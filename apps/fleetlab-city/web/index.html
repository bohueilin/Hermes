WHAT THIS CAN TEACH

A resource trade-off under stated assumptions.

Location can reduce empty travel while splitting capacity creates local queues. Read completion, wait, empty distance and neighborhood outcomes together.

WHAT THIS DOESN’T ESTABLISH

A deployment decision.

Demand and facilities are synthetic. The selected graph model has no endogenous traffic interactions. These results do not predict an operator’s service or real-world safety.

Method correction · 29 Sep 2026

The original lower-energy case records two no_reachable_depot violations per arm, not reserve_breach. Its lower-demand sensitivity is one seed (−1.5 pp), not a general outcome. Nominal capacity_kwh = 60 is unused metadata: the engine does not read it. Replay shows recorded kWh and the 48 kWh charging target; 30 kWh is the initial value in the evaluation studies. A DIFFERENT QUESTION FOR EVERY TEAM

What would you take into the next review?

Use this synthetic experiment to frame better questions. Operator outcomes still need calibrated data and qualified models.

01 / ENGINEERING

Can we reproduce it?

Same inputs, different depot policy. Trace resource ownership and queue duration; keep map gaps visible.

02 / PRODUCT

Did service improve?

Compare completed requests and passenger wait across all repeats, including uneven neighborhood outcomes.

03 / OPERATIONS

Where does the day go?

Separate passenger time from empty travel, depot queues, service and charging. A parked vehicle can reveal the bottleneck.

04 / FLEET MANAGEMENT

Is energy available where needed?

Inspect port access, charging energy and reserve constraints. Same total capacity can produce different local queues.

05 / SALES

Which assumptions drive value?

Apply your own fares to completed simulated trips. This is a gross-fare illustration, not an operator revenue forecast.

06 / DEPOT PARTNERSHIPS

What makes a site useful?

Test location together with power, ports and turnaround capacity. Shorter returns alone do not establish a viable site.
