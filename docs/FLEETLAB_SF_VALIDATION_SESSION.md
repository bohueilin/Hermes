# FleetLab San Francisco — independent visitor validation

Use the current public site: https://fleetlab.pages.dev/. Five visitors should
try it independently before seeing the suggested answers. Anonymous participant
labels P1–P5 are sufficient; do not collect personal details. Record actual
answers verbatim with consent. This is a formative learning check, not statistical
proof, safety validation or automatic launch approval.

## Session steps

1. Open City Explorer and let the visitor explore without coaching for five minutes.
2. Ask first: “What does this page conclude about depots?” Record the answer before any prompting.
3. Ask what a seed means and how a repeat differs from a driving scenario.
4. Ask what EV-001 through EV-100 represent and whether they are 100 different types of vehicle.
5. Ask what stays constant when one depot is compared with two.
6. Ask them to explain 394.5 queued vehicle-hours out of 800, and whether all non-queued time is passenger service.
7. Ask what the result establishes about real San Francisco service or vehicle safety.
8. Have them change a repeat/configuration, inspect a suggested vehicle, press Play, find map limitations and return to FleetLab.
9. Ask which wording or interaction was confusing; record assistance required and errors.
10. After the open responses above, ask what +1.11 percentage points, its interval
    and the +2-percentage-point practical threshold mean together. Record the
    visitor's words before explaining. This checks the October 4 clarity fix.

## Scoring, after the interview

Two scorers independently mark each item correct, partial, incorrect or unclear,
quote the supporting answer and reconcile disagreements visibly. Counts only:
no percentage confidence, population inference or automatic pass from n=5.

Expected concepts: a seed reproduces synthetic inputs; the 100 vehicle IDs share
one generic model and interact with common resources; the twelve paired repeats
are the experimental replication unit; total fleet, charging and turnaround
resources are controlled; queued time sums across vehicles; other states include
empty travel, charging and service; the study is synthetic and cannot establish
road safety, regulatory approval or an operator depot recommendation.
