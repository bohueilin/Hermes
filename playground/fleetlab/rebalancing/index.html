    WHY IT MATTERS / EXTERNAL RESEARCH

    Spatial availability
    is part of fleet readiness.

    Jacobsen, Kulcsár and Lindman's joint dispatch, rebalancing and charging preprint reports up to 36% lower median passenger wait and 27% lower rebalancing distance against its stated baselines. Those are external research results; this small lesson does not reproduce that optimizer, dataset or effect size.

    arXiv preprint · first submitted 2025, revised 2026 ↗

    What is fixed, and what does this model leave out?

    Twenty-four identical vehicles start A8/B8/C2/D6 on a two-kilometer square. A frozen tape contains 163 requests over twelve ten-minute intervals. Local pickup takes 60 seconds and 200 empty meters. Adjacent-zone travel takes four minutes; diagonal travel takes eight. Pickup deadlines are six minutes after request arrival.

    Reactive moves target expirations in the preceding interval. Forecast moves target perfect next-interval origin counts. Both retain at least one ready vehicle in a donating zone, but differing targets also change their reserves. This is a comparison of two transparent heuristics, not only a timing switch, and neither is an optimizer or a trained forecast.

    Dispatch is local: no automatic cross-zone pickup. Passenger trips move vehicles between zones. There is no pooling, street traffic, energy model, cost calibration, safety model or VOR event in this fixture. A forecast of nine requests does not imply nine simultaneous cars are necessary.

    The independent verifier checks mandatory event coverage, legal allocations, exact request accounting, distance, row coverage and common-input identity. A failed candidate is rejected; its metrics do not replace accepted results.

    A SHARED VOCABULARY

    Name the state.
    Define the denominator.

    Ready / idle
    Available for dispatch, at a zone and not executing a move.
    COD: Car on Drive
    Executing a pickup, passenger trip or empty repositioning move.
    VOR: Vehicle Off-Road
    Unavailable for dispatch: off the drive network, not off the pavement. Charging, updating and maintenance are examples. R1 schedules none of these activities.
    Deadhead ratio
    Empty distance ÷ total distance in matching units. Empty kilometers alone are not this ratio.
    Service rate
    Pickups within the observation and deadline ÷ admitted requests. Completed trips are reported separately.
    Readiness
    Operable-and-ready time ÷ scheduled time, with activity scope defined. This lesson does not calculate that score.

    Ready / idle, COD and VOR are FleetLab teaching labels, not a claim of universal AV-industry terminology.

    One word, several ratios

    Utilization can mean loaded distance ÷ total fleet distance, used link rate ÷ link capacity, or charger power draw ÷ power ceiling. Throughput needs units: bits/s, trips/h or vehicles/h. Service rate here is a served fraction; queueing theory also uses jobs completed per unit time. Name the resource behind capacity. Rebalancing here means empty interzone repositioning; local pickup approach is counted separately.
